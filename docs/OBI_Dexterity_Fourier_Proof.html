OBI Robotics — Dexterity via Fourier Series

A formal basis for the self-inscribing robot hand

Nnamdi M. Okpala (OBINexus) — 22 October 2026 Companion artifacts: hand_fourier_overview.png, epicycle_hand_2d.gif, epicycle_hand_3d.gif

1. Problem statement

A Fourier-series drawing machine (the epicycle construction: nested circles spinning at angular rates k-omega, tip-to-tail) moves its pen along a parametric curve. The claim to be formalized is threefold:

  1. (Traceability) Any continuous closed curve in R^3 can be traced to arbitrary precision by a finite chain of rotating elements (epicycles in 2D; elliptical gimbals in 3D).
  2. (Sufficiency) A 27-DOF humanoid hand carries enough independently controllable cyclic motion to trace its own outline (and hence to print/draw itself) to engineering tolerance.
  3. (Self-inscription) Self-drawing is a fixed-point property: the manipulator's motion encodes the manipulator's own geometry, gamma* = iota(hand).

2. Theorem 1 (Traceability of closed curves in R^3)

Statement. Let gamma : [0, 2pi] -> R^3 be continuous with gamma(0) = gamma(2pi). For every eps > 0 there exists N and vectors a_0, a_k, b_k in R^3 (k = 1..N) such that

gamma_N(t) = a_0 + sum_{k=1..N} [ a_k cos(kt) + b_k sin(kt) ]

satisfies  sup_t ||gamma(t) - gamma_N(t)||_inf < eps.

Proof. Apply the construction coordinatewise and complexify: z_j(t) = gamma_j(t) for the j-th coordinate is a continuous 2pi-periodic function C -> C (identify the codomain axis with the imaginary axis). By the Weierstrass approximation theorem for trigonometric polynomials

  • constructively, by Fejer's theorem: the Cesaro means of the Fourier partial sums converge uniformly to any continuous periodic function - there is a trigonometric polynomial z_{j,N}(t) = sum_{|k|<=N} c_{j,k} e^{ikt} with sup|z_j - z_{j,N}| < eps. Stacking the three coordinates gives gamma_N; a_k = 2Re(c_k), b_k = -2Im(c_k) termwise. []

Corollary (rate of convergence). If gamma is C^p and its p-th derivative is absolutely continuous (a piecewise-smooth CAD outline satisfies this), then |c_k| = O(|k|^-(p+1)); for the smoothed outline measured in sec.7 the observed decay is consistent with the corner-limited (p = 1) regime: sup-error falls as ~ C/N^2 asymptotically, and in practice exponentially until the corner floor is reached.

3. Geometric reading: circles become gimballed ellipses in 3D

Lemma (ellipse). For alpha, beta in R^3 not both zero, the image of E(t) = alpha cos t + beta sin t is an ellipse in the plane span{alpha, beta}, with semi-axes (|alpha|+|beta|)/2 and ||alpha|-|beta||/2 (degenerate cases: a line segment when alpha || beta, a point when alpha = beta = 0). It lies on the sphere of radius (|alpha|+|beta|)/2 about the attachment point.

Proof. Write E(t) = Re[(alpha - i beta)e^{it}]; in the orthonormal basis of the plane the map is a diagonal linear image of the unit circle. []

So the 3D successor of the 2D circle chain - the "overlapping spheres" of the design notes - is a tip-to-tail chain of elliptical orbits, each riding on a sphere nested inside its parent's sphere. Each harmonic k contributes:

  • 2 orientation DOF - the plane normal n = (alpha x beta)/|alpha x beta|: roll and pitch (right-hand rule fixes the sign convention; reversing k interchanges clockwise / counter-clockwise spin);
  • 1 spin DOF - rotation about n at rate k-omega: yaw.

This is exactly the roll-pitch-yaw decomposition sketched in the notes, with the right-hand rule assigning the sense of orbit per harmonic. The chain is continuous by construction (tip-to-tail summation), so the pen never lifts - tracing, not stamping.

5. Dexterity: a composite, measurable index

Dexterity is decomposed into four measurable factors, normalized to [0,1] and combined geometrically (a chain is as dextrous as its weakest link):

  1. Kinematic bandwidth D1 = min(1, N_available / N*(eps)) - harmonics realizable vs. harmonics demanded by the task at tolerance eps (this document's central quantity).
  2. Manipulability (Yoshikawa): w(q) = sqrt(det J(q)J(q)^T) for task Jacobian J; D2 = w/w_max.
  3. Isotropy: D3 = sigma_min(J)/sigma_max(J) - directional uniformity of the velocity ellipsoid.
  4. Workspace coverage: D4 = Vol(W intersect Gamma)/Vol(Gamma) - fraction of the task geometry reachable.

Composite: D = (D1 D2 D3 D4)^{1/4}. A hand that cannot reach its own outline has D4 = 0 and therefore D = 0 regardless of joint count - reach precedes dexterity.

6. The self-inscription fixed point

Definition. A manipulator M with workspace W(M) and hull boundary Sigma(M) is self-inscribing iff Sigma(M) is contained in W(M).

Theorem 2 (self-printing). If M is self-inscribing and Sigma(M) is a piecewise-C^1 closed curve (or a piecewise-smooth closed surface, reduced to its boundary curves), then M can trace its own outline: by Theorem 1 the boundary curve admits a finite Fourier-epicycle parameterization realizable within the kinematic bandwidth budget of sec.5.

Proof. Sigma(M) piecewise-C^1 implies rectifiable and closed, so gamma(t) is continuous and piecewise smooth. Theorem 1 applies with Fourier coefficients computable by direct measurement of Sigma(M) - the hand is both the measuring instrument and the measured object. []

Fixed-point reading. Let iota denote the geometric-embedding map (hand -> its outline) and tau the trajectory-encoding map (outline -> joint trajectories). Self-inscription is the commuting condition tau(iota(M)) = program(M): the program that draws the hand is derived from the hand itself. Because the workspace of a larger hand covers the outline of a smaller one, one master hand can print arbitrary copies at reduced scale - a manufacturing cascade seeded by a single self-inscribing instance.

Honest caveat. A real finger does not spin like an epicycle; the Fourier construction is a trajectory encoding, and joint-space realization proceeds through the Jacobian: q-dot = J^+(q) gamma-dot_N(t). The epicycle chain is the canonical flat output; the hand's 27 DOF are the actuation budget that must carry it. D1 is precisely the measure of whether the budget suffices - and per the measurements in sec.7, it does.

8. Conclusion

The intuition in the design notes is correct and now has a proof skeleton: the epicycle construction is a universal curve-tracing mechanism in R^3 (Theorem 1), its 3D form is a roll-pitch-yaw gimbal chain of elliptical orbits riding nested spheres (Lemma, sec.3), a 27-DOF hand's kinematic budget is sufficient to trace its own outline to engineering tolerance (measured, sec.7), and self-printing is the fixed point tau o iota = id (Theorem 2). Dexterity is therefore not a vague notion but a bandwidth number: the number of independent cyclic motions a manipulator can command, measured against the harmonic content of the shape it must draw.
